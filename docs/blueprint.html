Problem Framing

Plan and simulate an autonomous car that drives safely on Indian roads with no lane discipline. It shares the road with cars, buses, trucks, auto-rickshaws, two-wheelers, bicycles, pushcarts, pedestrians and cattle, often on roads with no markings, blurred edges and potholes. The car has to perceive (camera, LiDAR, radar), predict irregular, non-lane motion, plan and replan a collision-free path in real time, and prove it across at least five realistic scenarios using replanning latency, path smoothness and completion rate.

Where We Are Now
The full autonomy stack already runs closed-loop in Python. It covers sensor models with occlusion, EKF fusion, multi-modal prediction, a decision FSM, A* rerouting, a lane-free Frenet lattice planner and a bicycle-model car. It completed 47 of 48 randomised runs across 6 scenarios with 0 collisions. This blueprint covers the next step: moving that stack into a 3D Gazebo digital twin with ROS 2, and keeping the MathWorks deliverables covered.
Three Challenges → Three Modules

Perceive

Camera (class), LiDAR (position, size, heading) and radar (range, radial velocity) are fused into one track list by an EKF with Hungarian association. Objects hidden behind buses, bushes and buildings stay invisible until they come out.

Predict

Each track gets a constant-velocity path with class-specific uncertainty growth, which the car must avoid. It also gets possible stop, swerve and dart-into-road moves, which only raise cost. Traffic behind a stopped vehicle is predicted to queue, not drive through it.

Plan & Replan

About 500–1,100 candidate paths are sampled across the whole road width. Planning runs every 0.2 s, plus immediately on new objects or broken plans. A* reroutes when static objects block the road.

What "Full-Fledged" Means Here
  • A working perception → prediction → planning → decision → vehicle pipeline, closed-loop (done in Python; Simulink port for the MathWorks submission)
  • At least 5 Indian scenarios, with two detailed 3D scenes (village road, urban intersection). This is where the Gazebo twin and RoadRunner come in
  • Metrics: replanning latency, path smoothness, completion rate, plus clearance, time-to-collision and reaction latency
  • A short technical report and a demonstration video (both drafted; the 3D twin makes the video far stronger)
Is This Format Better?

Yes, for presenting and planning. A tabbed blueprint lets judges and your team jump straight to architecture, results or the demo plan. A single long report hides that. Use this page as the PPT source and the internal-round handout, and keep REPORT.md as the formal technical report.

Gazebo is a strong addition but not a replacement. This problem statement's expected solution asks for a MATLAB/Simulink pipeline and two RoadRunner scenes. Gazebo on its own risks being judged off-spec. The plan below uses both, sharing one autonomy stack.

What the Gazebo Twin Adds

Real sensor data

Rendered camera frames and simulated LiDAR point clouds replace the idealised detection lists. A YOLO model then has to actually find the auto-rickshaw in pixels, including at dusk and when it is half hidden behind a bus.

Physics

The ego car is a real 4-wheel rigid body with Ackermann steering, tyre friction and suspension. Potholes, speed breakers and camber become physical, not just a cost term.

3D demo footage

A chase-camera and bird's-eye view of a car weaving past a cow on a village road is a far stronger demo video than 2D plots.

Sim-to-real path

The same ROS 2 graph can later drive a scale-model car or a Jetson-based UGV without changing node code.

Build Roads Without Lane Markings

Reuse the road network you already have. python3 export_matlab.py writes road.json for every scenario (nodes, edges, widths, potholes, buildings). A small generator turns it into SDF:

  • Road surface: one thin box per edge segment, with an asphalt or packed-earth texture and no painted lines. Put a 0.5 m dusty shoulder strip either side so the edge is visually blurred, which is what makes edge estimation hard.
  • Potholes: short cylinders sunk 5–8 cm into a slightly raised road mesh, or a heightmap for the village road. The car feels them physically through the suspension.
  • Occluders: the same polygons as world.occluders become shop rows, compound walls and bushes. Camera and LiDAR occlusion now happens for real, not by ray-casting maths.
  • Village terrain: a heightmap (DEM tile or a Blender-sculpted ditch) under the road gives camber and uneven shoulders.
  • Lighting: one world file per time of day (<sun> direction plus ambient) to stress the camera at noon glare and dusk.
bharat_worlds/scripts/gen_world.py (core idea)
import json, math, sys
road = json.load(open(sys.argv[1]))                 # matlab/data/S1_village/road.json
out = []
for e in road["edges"]:
    pts = e["pts"]
    for (x0, y0), (x1, y1) in zip(pts[:-1], pts[1:]):
        L = math.hypot(x1-x0, y1-y0); yaw = math.atan2(y1-y0, x1-x0)
        cx, cy = (x0+x1)/2, (y0+y1)/2
        out.append(f'''<model name="road_{len(out)}"><static>true</static>
  <pose>{cx} {cy} 0.02 0 0 {yaw}</pose>
  <link name="l"><collision name="c"><geometry><box><size>{L+0.4} {e["width"]} 0.04</size></box></geometry></collision>
  <visual name="v"><geometry><box><size>{L+0.4} {e["width"]} 0.04</size></box></geometry>
  <material><diffuse>0.36 0.36 0.35 1</diffuse></material></visual></link></model>''')
# + potholes, occluder polygons (extruded boxes), sun/lighting, then wrap in <world>
print("\n".join(out))

The Ego Car (model.sdf)

A hatchback-sized rigid body: 4.4 m × 1.8 m, wheelbase 2.7 m, about 1,100 kg. It matches the Python bicycle model so the planner's limits (33° steering, 3.2 m/s² lateral) stay valid. Steering uses the Ackermann system; the car listens to a Twist on its cmd_vel topic.

Radar: Gazebo has no built-in automotive radar sensor. Emulate it with a radar_emulator node. This is the same maths as sensors.py. Say this openly in the report.

Traffic Manager: Reuse Your world.py Behaviours

Don't hand-script every vehicle in SDF. Run the same World / PathFollow / Crosser / Wander classes in a ROS 2 node at 20 Hz. Feed it the real ego pose from Gazebo and teleport each agent model to its new pose. Agents keep their Indian behaviours (wander, informal merges, darting, cattle pausing), and scenario seeds stay reproducible.

bharat_av/nodes/traffic_manager.py (skeleton)
import rclpy
from rclpy.node import Node
from nav_msgs.msg import Odometry
from gz.transport13 import Node as GzNode          # Harmonic python bindings
from gz.msgs10.pose_pb2 import Pose
from gz.msgs10.boolean_pb2 import Boolean
from bharat_av.av.scenarios import SCENARIOS

class TrafficManager(Node):
    def __init__(self):
        super().__init__("traffic_manager")
        self.declare_parameter("scenario", "S5"); self.declare_parameter("seed", 0)
        spec = SCENARIOS[self.get_parameter("scenario").value](self.get_parameter("seed").value)
        self.world = spec.world
        self.world.ego = EgoProxy()                      # x, y, yaw, v, L, W updated from odom
        for a in self.world.agents:
            if a.behavior: a.behavior.place(a)
        self.gz = GzNode()
        self.create_subscription(Odometry, "/ego/odom", self.world.ego.update, 10)
        self.create_timer(0.05, self.tick)

    def tick(self):
        self.world.step(0.05)
        for a in self.world.agents:
            req = Pose(); req.name = f"agent_{a.id}"
            req.position.x, req.position.y = a.x, a.y
            req.orientation.z, req.orientation.w = math.sin(a.yaw/2), math.cos(a.yaw/2)
            self.gz.request("/world/indian_roads/set_pose", req, Pose, Boolean, 50)
# spawn one model per agent at launch (ros_gz_sim create) named agent_<id>
Teleported agents are kinematic: they don't get pushed by physics. That's fine and even desirable, because they behave exactly as scripted. Collisions are measured by metrics_logger from ground-truth poses with the same rectangle test used in Python. With 20–40 agents, one set_pose call per agent per tick is fine. If the real-time factor drops, batch the calls or lower the tick rate to 10 Hz.

Wrapping an Existing Module (pattern)
bharat_av/nodes/lattice_planner.py (pattern)
from bharat_av.av.planner import LatticePlanner     # unchanged Python module

class PlannerNode(Node):
    def __init__(self):
        super().__init__("lattice_planner")
        self.planner = LatticePlanner()
        self.create_subscription(PredictionArray, "/predictions", self.on_pred, 10)
        self.create_subscription(Behavior, "/behavior", self.on_beh, 10)
        self.create_timer(0.2, self.periodic)             # 5 Hz periodic
        self.pub = self.create_publisher(Path, "/plan", 10)

    def on_pred(self, msg):
        self.hyps = from_msg(msg)                          # → list[Hypothesis]
        if not self.planner.still_valid(self.plan, self.hyps, self.t_since_plan()):
            self.replan("plan_invalid")                     # event-triggered, same as sim.py
Define small custom messages (Track, Hypothesis, Behavior) in a bharat_msgs package. Keep the planner's inner loop in NumPy. It already runs at 20–60 ms on average, well inside the 200 ms cycle.
Camera Detection for Indian Classes

Model

YOLO (Ultralytics, small variant for speed) with classes: car, bus, truck, tractor, auto-rickshaw, motorcycle, bicycle, person, animal/cattle, pushcart. It publishes a bounding box, class and confidence. Range comes from LiDAR points inside the box (frustum association).

Data

  • IDD, the India Driving Dataset (IIIT Hyderabad): Indian roads, includes auto-rickshaws and animals
  • Synthetic frames from your own Gazebo worlds, auto-labelled from ground-truth poses (free, perfectly labelled, covers dusk and fog)
  • Fine-tune on the mix; report mAP on held-out IDD and synthetic data
LiDAR & Radar
  • LiDAR clusterer: remove the ground plane (RANSAC or height threshold), Euclidean clustering, then an L-shape box fit for size and heading. Heading from box fitting is what fixed the long-bus yaw issue in the Python stack.
  • Radar emulator: the same noise model as sensors.py. It gives long range and radial velocity, and sees pedestrians and cattle poorly.
  • Fusion: tracker.py unchanged. The inputs are now real detections instead of sampled ones, so expect lower camera recall at dusk. That is the point of the test.
Keep the Python sensor models as a fast "perfect-perception minus noise" baseline. Report both. The gap between them shows how much perception quality costs the planner, and judges tend to ask about that.
